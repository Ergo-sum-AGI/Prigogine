1. Large-$t$ Asymptotics of $I_{CC}(t)$
Consider the continuum–continuum ($CC$) sector contribution to Prigogine's Lyapunov functional $\Omega(t)$ along the deformed contour $\zeta = x e^{-i\theta}$, $\zeta' = x' e^{-i\theta}$ ($\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$ fixed): [ I_{CC}(t) := \iint_{(0,\infty)^2} e^{-2(x+x')\sin\theta, t} \left| \langle\langle \Psi_{xe^{-i\theta}, x'e^{-i\theta}}^L \mid \rho_0 \rangle\rangle \right|^2 , dx , dx'. ] Suppose the initial state $\rho_0$ has threshold/near-origin pairing behavior [ \left| \langle\langle \Psi_{xe^{-i\theta}, x'e^{-i\theta}}^L \mid \rho_0 \rangle\rangle \right| \sim c_0 (x x')^{\alpha/2} \quad \text{as } x, x' \to 0^+ ] (for $\alpha > -1$ so the pairing is locally integrable near the threshold), with decay/regularization at infinity ensuring convergence of the integral.
1.1 Scaling and Asymptotic Evaluation
Set $\mu := 2\sin\theta > 0$ and introduce the rescaled variables [ u = \mu t x, \qquad v = \mu t x', \qquad dx,dx' = (\mu t)^{-2},du,dv. ] Substituting into $I_{CC}(t)$ gives: [ I_{CC}(t) = \iint_{(0,\infty)^2} e^{-(u+v)} \left| \langle\langle \Psi_{\frac{u}{\mu t}e^{-i\theta}, \frac{v}{\mu t}e^{-i\theta}}^L \mid \rho_0 \rangle\rangle \right|^2 \frac{du,dv}{(\mu t)^2}. ] Using the near-origin scaling $\left|\langle\langle \Psi_{xe^{-i\theta},x'e^{-i\theta}}^L \mid \rho_0 \rangle\rangle\right|^2 \sim c_0^2 (x x')^\alpha$ for large $t$: [ I_{CC}(t) \sim c_0^2 \iint_{(0,\infty)^2} e^{-(u+v)} \left(\frac{u}{\mu t}\right)^\alpha \left(\frac{v}{\mu t}\right)^\alpha \frac{du,dv}{(\mu t)^2} = \frac{c_0^2}{(\mu t)^{2\alpha + 2}} \left( \int_0^\infty e^{-u} u^\alpha , du \right)^2. ] Evaluating the Gamma integrals $\int_0^\infty e^{-u} u^\alpha , du = \Gamma(\alpha + 1)$ (for $\alpha > -1$) yields: [ I_{CC}(t) \sim C_\alpha , t^{-2(\alpha + 1)}, \qquad C_\alpha := \frac{c_0^2 , \Gamma(\alpha + 1)^2}{(2\sin\theta)^{2\alpha + 2}}. ] (Note: If the integrand measure in a given convention has an extra factor of $t$—for instance, from a Jacobian or density weighting—the exponent shifts by $+1$ to $t^{-(2\alpha+1)}$ or $t^{-\alpha}$; under the explicit $dx,dx'$ measure written above, the exact power-law exponent is $2(\alpha + 1)$.)


2. Competition with Exponential Resonance Decay $e^{-2\gamma t}$ and Crossover Time $t_*(\alpha, \gamma)$
An isolated resonance pole with imaginary part $-\gamma$ ($\gamma > 0$) contributes an exponential decay term of the form [ I_{\mathrm{res}}(t) \sim A_{\mathrm{res}} , e^{-2\gamma t}. ] Comparing the exponential resonance term $A_{\mathrm{res}} e^{-2\gamma t}$ with the continuum power law $C_\alpha t^{-2(\alpha+1)}$:

For any $\alpha > -1$ and $\gamma > 0$, as $t \to \infty$: Because $e^{-2\gamma t}$ decays exponentially while $t^{-2(\alpha+1)}$ decays only algebraically, $I_{CC}(t)$ always overtakes $I_{\mathrm{res}}(t)$ at sufficiently large $t$: [ \lim_{t\to\infty} \frac{I_{\mathrm{res}}(t)}{I_{CC}(t)} = 0. ]
Crossover time $t_*(\alpha,\gamma)$: Setting $A_{\mathrm{res}} e^{-2\gamma t_} = C_\alpha t_^{-2(\alpha+1)}$ gives the transcendental relation [ t_^{\alpha+1} e^{-\gamma t_} = \sqrt{\frac{C_\alpha}{A_{\mathrm{res}}}}, ] which in terms of the Lambert $W$ function (for $C_\alpha / A_{\mathrm{res}}$ sufficiently small so the regime is reached at large $t$) scales asymptotically as [ t_*(\alpha, \gamma) \sim \frac{\alpha + 1}{\gamma} \ln!\left(\frac{\gamma}{\alpha + 1}\right) + \frac{1}{2\gamma}\ln!\left(\frac{A_{\mathrm{res}}}{C_\alpha}\right) \qquad (\text{as } A_{\mathrm{res}}/C_\alpha \text{ or } \gamma \text{ varies}). ]
